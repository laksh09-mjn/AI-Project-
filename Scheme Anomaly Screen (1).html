    What the screen looks for

    Each signal is scaled from 0 to 1 and the signals are merged so that several weak hints add up. Scheme medians, not one global threshold, decide what counts as a large payment.

    Unusual amount

    Payment far above the typical amount for its own scheme, measured with a robust z-score on the logarithm of the amount.

    Repeated payments

    Same vendor and amount several times within 15 days. Catches duplicates and bills split to stay under an approval limit.

    Round amounts

    Large payments of exactly ₹50,000, ₹1,00,000 and similar. Real invoices rarely land on round figures.

    Year-end rush

    Large payments in the last week of March, when budgets are used up before the financial year closes.

    Vendor concentration

    One vendor holding an unusually large share of a district's spending.

    Isolation Forest and Benford

    An unsupervised model scores records that look odd across all features together, including a first-digit (Benford) deviation per vendor.

    A flag is a lead, not a verdict

    Unusual spending can have honest causes, such as a large one-off project. Auditors confirm or dismiss every flag. Published data can be incomplete, so results depend on its quality.
